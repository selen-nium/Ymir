The problem

The answers are in the drawings. Finding them takes hours.

A building is described by dozens of sheets, each from a different discipline. Engineers flip between them to answer simple questions, and a tool that guesses without showing its work can’t be trusted on site.

Spread across sheets

How long are the footings under the outside walls? That takes the foundation plan, the floor plan and a schedule, drawn by three disciplines.

Answers without evidence

A number without a source is a liability. Engineers need to see which room, which wall and which sheet it came from, and how sure the model is.

Every set is different

Scanned archives, CAD exports and IFC models; Finnish, German and Danish labels; metric and imperial. One fixed pipeline doesn’t fit them all.

What Ymir does

Every answer comes with its evidence. Every gap is shown.

Ymir treats a question like an engineer would: find the right sheet, measure or count, check the result, then show where it came from.

How it works

From a scanned sheet to a cited answer

A static frontend talks straight to a Python API. The build pipeline turns images into geometry once; the agents answer each question on top of it and stream their trace back.

Upload

A sheet of any kind, or a whole set. Samples come rendered from IFC models.

Build

Walls, rooms, text and scale are read from the image. You confirm what the model isn’t sure about.

Assemble

Sheets take their place in one building. What’s missing becomes a ghost.

Ask

The planner routes the question to tools; the verifier checks; the answer cites its sources.
